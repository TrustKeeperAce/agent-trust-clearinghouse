Summary

Julius AI is a commercial AI data-analysis tool that generates and runs code from natural-language prompts. Four limited independent evaluations were found. A 2026 peer-reviewed study found high accuracy on text-based statistics questions (86.49% on a statistical-literacy test; 78.6% on statistical data questions) and lower accuracy on causal questions and items needing figures. Two education studies from one research group found errors on a PDF math worksheet and a multiplication prompt, and a peer-reviewed comparison tested Julius with code execution disabled. Informational only; not a guarantee, endorsement, or certification.

Overall score: 59 · Evidence grade: B- · Independent sources: 4 performance (2 peer-reviewed), 5 other

Evidence grade describes the amount and type of evidence, not the agent's quality. Grade B-: 4 counted sources (3–4 band; see methodology). Three of the four performance sources are limited (code execution disabled; a single prompt; a PDF worksheet in a venue whose review could not be confirmed).

Disclosures for counted sources

  • Quality & Quantity (2026): the authors state that the vendor provided complimentary access to the tool during the initial stages of the work. No vendor authors; no conflicts declared.
  • IJRSI (2026) and the Ohio Journal of School Mathematics study (2025) share three authors (Sawyer, Aga, Sutherland). They are counted as separate studies because they use different tasks, dates and tool sets.

Uncertainties

  • Only one counted study (Quality & Quantity 2026) tests Julius on data-analysis tasks as normally used; it does not state the version, underlying model or test date, and the vendor gave the authors complimentary access.
  • The IJRSI study is in a venue whose review could not be confirmed (accepted 5 days after receipt), tests a PDF mathematics worksheet rather than a data file, and shares three authors with the Ohio Journal of School Mathematics study.
  • The Applied Sciences comparison contains internal inconsistencies in its stated test dates and software versions.
  • Benchmarks published by competing products are not independent.
  • Privacy, retention, training-use, and compliance statements are vendor statements and were not independently verified.
  • The product and its billing model changed in 2026; no counted source tests the 2026 product, and older evidence may not reflect it.
  • A 2025 Teaching Statistics illustration (Vilakati) is listed but not counted; the line between a documented hands-on test and an illustration is a judgment call.
