Summary

Gemini CLI is Google's open-source coding agent for the terminal, normally run with Gemini models. 28 independent studies that ran it were counted, 8 of them peer-reviewed (including ICLR, ISSTA, AAAI and ASE 2026), and 18 state the version tested, from 0.6.1 (2025) to 0.61.0 (October 2026). Task results were mixed: with the same model, Gemini CLI was level with or above some other harnesses and well below others. Many counted studies attacked Gemini CLI, and planted instructions often succeeded; several found it more exposed than other harnesses tested. Google fixed a prompt-injection flaw found by Tracebit in v0.1.14 (July 2025) and two 2026 flaws in 0.39.1 (CVE-2026-12537, critical by Google's score, and CVE-2026-13745); for the second, Google issued a CVE only after the reporter disputed its refusal. The A grade rests on a single peer-reviewed study that states a recent version (v0.27.3), published in a lower-assurance education journal; without it the entry would be graded B with an overall score of 51. Studies by Google authors are not counted. Informational only; not a guarantee, endorsement, or certification.

Overall score: 53 · Evidence grade: A · Independent sources: 28 performance (8 peer-reviewed), 13 other

Scope

Gemini CLI from the google-gemini/gemini-cli repository, from its initial release (v0.1.0, June 2025) through 0.65.0 (20 October 2026), in interactive and headless (non-interactive) use, run with any model. Advisories for the run-gemini-cli GitHub Action are described where they also affect Gemini CLI. Google Jules (a separate product with its own entry), Gemini Code Assist agent mode in IDEs, other Google agents, systems that use Gemini CLI as one part of a larger agent, and results for Gemini models alone are not covered. Results depend heavily on the language model, the configuration and the task.

What this entry does not cover

Google Jules is a separate Google coding agent that works in the cloud; it has its own entry, and its results are not counted here. Gemini Code Assist agent mode in IDEs is also a separate product. Benchmarks that test Gemini models on their own, without Gemini CLI, describe the model, not this agent, and are not counted.

Model dependence

Gemini CLI is designed for Google's Gemini models, and most studies ran it with one of them, so each result is for Gemini CLI together with a particular model and settings. With the same model, studies found Gemini CLI level with or above some harnesses (TerminalWorld, FeatureBench, SkillsBench) and well below others (Terminal-Bench 2.0). A few studies ran it with other companies' models through adapters; those results may not carry over to everyday use. Results for one model do not carry over to another.

How sources were counted

Google's own documentation, repository and announcements, and studies with Google or Google DeepMind authors, are not counted. Studies that ran Gemini CLI itself count once each, including fixed-harness benchmarks and security studies that attack it (see Decision 0087, D3). Studies whose authors' own coding product is in the comparison are listed but not counted. Systems that use Gemini CLI as one part of a larger agent, source-code taxonomies and repository-mining studies are listed as other or not counted. Security advisories and incident reports are described under disputes and are not counted as tests.

Security and disputes

In June 2025 Sam Cox of Tracebit reported that a prompt injection hidden in a project file, combined with weak matching of allowed shell commands and whitespace that hid the rest of a command, could make Gemini CLI silently run attacker commands after a user had allowed a harmless one. According to Tracebit, Google initially triaged the report as low priority, raised it to the highest priority on 23 July 2025 and fixed it in v0.1.14 on 25 July 2025; Tracebit published on 28 July. No CVE was assigned. CVE-2026-12537 (critical by Google's CVSS 4.0 score of 10.0; NVD 7.8), published on 24 April 2026: in headless mode Gemini CLI skipped the workspace trust check, so a planted configuration file could run code before the sandbox started, and the --yolo option ignored tool allowlists. It affects Gemini CLI before 0.39.1 and the run-gemini-cli GitHub Action before 0.1.22, and is fixed in 0.39.1, 0.40.0-preview.3 and 0.1.22. Pillar Security, which showed that a malicious GitHub issue could compromise the gemini-cli repository's own automation, reports that Google patched within two days and thanks it for its cooperation. CVE-2026-13745 (high, 7.7), published on 10 September 2026: a .env file in an untrusted folder could bypass the folder-trust prompt and run code; fixed in 0.39.1. According to the reporter, Google paid a US$500 bounty but twice declined to issue a CVE, agreeing only after a dispute was opened with MITRE; the record was corrected on 23 September 2026. Cyera reported two further command-injection flaws in 2025, fixed through Google's reward program. In July 2025 a user reported that Gemini CLI had deleted project files, which was widely reported; the user later wrote that the files had been found elsewhere on the drive, so they were misplaced rather than deleted. No lawsuit or regulatory action concerning Gemini CLI was found.

Uncertainties

  • Results reflect the model and the harness together; most studies ran Gemini CLI with Gemini models, so differences may come from either.
  • Gemini CLI releases weekly (0.65.0 on 20 October 2026); no counted test names a version after 0.61.0, and security behaviour such as folder trust changed across versions.
  • 12 of the 28 counted studies are attack studies, often run with confirmations off or in headless mode; they describe Gemini CLI under attack, not in everyday use.
  • The Tracebit flaw (fixed in v0.1.14) has no CVE, and the two 2026 CVEs have different severity scores from Google and from NVD.
  • Google Jules and Gemini Code Assist agent mode are separate products; results for them are not counted here.
