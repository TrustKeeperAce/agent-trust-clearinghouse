Summary

SAFE (Search-Augmented Factuality Evaluator) is open research code from Google DeepMind, described in a NeurIPS 2024 paper, that uses a language model and Google Search results to rate individual facts in long-form text. The authors report 72% agreement with crowdsourced annotators. Independent peer-reviewed studies report agreement with human annotators of about 84% on biography facts and strong correlation on factual labels, alongside weaker results for its claim extraction; these studies were conducted by researchers proposing alternative methods. Commentators have questioned the authors' comparison with crowdworkers rather than expert fact-checkers. Overall score 66. Informational only; not a guarantee, endorsement, or certification.

Uncertainties

  • Results depend on which language model SAFE runs with and on the Serper search API.
  • Cost figures across studies are not directly comparable.
  • All three independent studies come from researchers proposing competing evaluators.
  • No independent study using expert fact-checkers was found.
  • The authors' 72% and 76% figures are their own claims, not independent evidence.
  • The overall score is exactly 65.5; exact .5 rounds up (Decision 0010).
