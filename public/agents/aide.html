Summary

AIDE is an open-source agent from Weco AI that tackles machine-learning tasks, such as Kaggle competitions, by writing, running and refining candidate solutions in a search tree. It is run with a language model chosen by the user. Thirteen independent studies that ran it were found (six peer-reviewed); most of them propose a competing method and use AIDE as a baseline. Results depend heavily on the model and task. On OpenAI's MLE-bench, o1-preview with AIDE earned a medal in 16.9% of 75 Kaggle competitions, and GPT-4o with AIDE did better than with two general-purpose agents (8.7% vs 0.8% and 4.4%). Several newer methods report better results than AIDE (for example 55% vs 66% of teams beaten on MLE-bench Lite), and one benchmark found it behind simpler search strategies. Studies of how it works found that 85-88% of its pipelines reported only a single evaluation metric, and that its pipelines were well below expert solutions on fairness. No CVE or legal action was found. No test from the last 12 months states the AIDE version. Informational only; not a guarantee, endorsement, or certification.

Scope

The open-source AIDE agent (aideml), which searches a tree of candidate Python solutions for a machine-learning task, from v0.1.x (April 2024) through v0.2.2 (5 November 2025) and the main branch (last commit 16 October 2026). Re-implementations of AIDE inside other frameworks (for example Meta's AIRA-dojo) and Weco's commercial platform are not covered. Results depend heavily on the language model, task and compute budget.

How sources were counted

Weco AI's own reports are not counted. Studies with AIDE developers as co-authors are excluded (FML-bench). Re-implementations of AIDE inside other frameworks, and papers that only reuse published AIDE numbers or trajectories, are listed as other sources. Most counted studies propose a competing method and use AIDE as a baseline; they count with disclosure. AutoKaggle, a separate project from different developers, has its own entry.

Security

AIDE runs code written by the model on the user's machine or in a container. No CVE for AIDE was found. A vulnerable dependency (datasets) was patched in October 2026. No legal or regulatory action was found.

Sources

Independent performance sources (counted)

  • MLE-bench (OpenAI; ICLR 2025): 75 Kaggle competitions; o1-preview + AIDE medal in 16.9%; GPT-4o + AIDE 8.7% vs MLAB 0.8% and OpenHands 4.4%. Disclosure: OpenAI supplies models evaluated and maintains the MLE-bench leaderboard — accessed 2026-10-17
  • CTRL-ALT-DECEIT (LawZero, Apollo Research, Imperial College London et al.; NeurIPS 2025): Claude 3.7 with AIDE just above the human median on main tasks, ahead of ReAct; when instructed, agents sometimes sabotaged or sandbagged without detection, though monitors were overall highly effective — accessed 2026-10-17
  • CO-Bench (Carnegie Mellon University; AAAI 2026): combinatorial optimisation algorithm search; AIDE below simpler strategies such as BestOfN on most metrics, with higher valid-solution rates — accessed 2026-10-17
  • InnoGym (Zhejiang University, Ant Group et al.; ICLR 2026): AIDE lowest of three frameworks (MLAB, CodeAct, AIDE) on its novelty measure (23.33 vs 39.58 for MLAB). Disclosure: authors overlap with a group building competing agents — accessed 2026-10-17
  • Can LLM Agents Discover? Evaluating Creativity on ML Engineering Tasks (COLM 2026): AIDE as the agent framework; attempt success 45.0% (GPT-5) and 26.2% (Qwen3-32B); exploration declined over a run in favour of parameter tuning — accessed 2026-10-17
  • SPIO (Enhans, Peking University, Yale; ACL 2026): tabular data-science tasks; AIDE comparable to other baselines (for example Titanic accuracy 0.7775 with GPT-4o), below SPIO (0.7871). Disclosure: proposes a competing method — accessed 2026-10-17
  • TimeSeriesGym (preprint 2025): AIDE with GPT-4.1 produced valid submissions on 57.3% of challenges and reasonable ones on 12.5%; AIDE outperformed OpenHands as a scaffold — accessed 2026-10-17
  • ML-Dev-Bench (preprint 2025): 30 ML development tasks; AIDE + GPT-4o 17% success (OpenHands + Claude 3.5 Sonnet 50%); AIDE is built for Kaggle-style tasks — accessed 2026-10-17
  • MARS (Google; preprint 2026): controlled MLE-bench Lite runs; AIDE any-medal 36.4% (Gemini 2.5 Pro) and 53.0% (Gemini 3 Pro Preview), below AIRA-dojo and MARS. Disclosure: proposes a competing method; Google supplies the models — accessed 2026-10-17
  • ArchPilot (Meta, UIUC; preprint Nov 2025): all 75 MLE-bench tasks under the same budget; AIDE valid submissions 78.7%, beat 55% of teams on MLE-bench Lite (ArchPilot 66%). Disclosure: proposes a competing method — accessed 2026-10-17
  • Simple Baselines are Competitive with Code Evolution (preprint, v2 Oct 2026): on 10 MLE-bench competitions with Gemini, a simple sequential search matched or beat AIDE on 6 of 10 — accessed 2026-10-17
  • Be Fair! (preprint 2026): dermatology classification with fairness instructions; AIDE higher AUC and smaller fairness gap than MLZero with about 6M vs 86M tokens, but all agent pipelines at least 10 AUC points below expert solutions — accessed 2026-10-17
  • The Silent Failures in Agentic Data Science (CWI Amsterdam; preprint Oct 2026): most agent pipelines had at least one data-science failure mode; AIDE removed missing validation splits but reported a single evaluation metric in 85-88% of pipelines — accessed 2026-10-17
